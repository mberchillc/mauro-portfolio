Editorial manifesto

Technology is never only technical

Studying technology to explain reality.

When algorithms began to occupy a central place in public life, I understood that society could no longer be explained without understanding the technological systems that were starting to organize it.

My work begins with a simple premise: communicating technology requires more than observing it from the outside. It means using it, researching it, studying it, teaching it and understanding how it changes human experience.

Technology rarely makes itself visible. Software, artificial intelligence models, data systems and algorithms operate behind interfaces. Journalism must expose that infrastructure and explain who designs it, which interests drive it, what decisions it automates and what consequences it creates.

That is what I mean by technological humanism: using journalism, the humanities, law, history and culture to interpret applied science. It means avoiding fascination with technique for its own sake without denying the value that technological progress can bring.

Tech & Society was created to offer a second reading: a place to pause, connect events, investigate sources and study technology as a way of explaining reality.
